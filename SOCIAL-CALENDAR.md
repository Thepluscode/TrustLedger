# TrustLedger Premise Exposure Calendar

**Period:** 17 August–15 September 2026  
**Primary platform:** LinkedIn  
**Secondary platform:** X  
**Research channel:** payment, fintech and reconciliation operator communities  
**Goal:** convert public attention into qualified incident-reconstruction conversations, operator
referrals, scoped sample data and paid discovery—not likes, generic product interest or feature
praise.

## Brand context

- **Audience:** Heads of Payments, reconciliation and settlement operators, payment operations
  leaders, payments engineers and finance-control leaders at organisations operating across two or
  more providers, rails or banks.
- **Voice:** founder-led, technically credible, specific, curious and willing to discover that the
  premise is wrong.
- **Canonical question:** **What actually happened to the money?**
- **Position:** TrustLedger is testing a read-only payment-reliability layer. It is not presented as
  a bank, gateway, custody product, ledger replacement, fraud platform or autonomous decision-maker.
- **Current proof boundary:** substantial repository engineering; zero completed market interviews,
  zero customer datasets and zero paid commitments at campaign start.

## Distribution decision

LinkedIn is the primary channel because the target is a cross-functional B2B buying group, from
operators and engineers to accountable payment leaders. X is used for concise technical questions,
public replies and payments-engineering discovery. Specialist communities are used sparingly and
only where their rules permit research requests.

Do not use Instagram, TikTok or paid advertising during this premise test. Broad reach would produce
weak signals while increasing the cost of distinguishing operators from spectators.

## Content pillars

1. **Incident education — 35%:** explain the operational anatomy of missing, late, duplicated and
   mismatched money without pitching the product.
2. **Founder learning — 20%:** expose the commercial uncertainty and what evidence would change the
   decision.
3. **Truth and safety policy — 20%:** preserve ambiguity, raw sources and accountable human outcomes.
4. **Operator participation — 15%:** questions, polls and research invitations that request a real
   incident or referral.
5. **Engineering proof with boundaries — 10%:** show why the premise is technically credible without
   converting tests into customer or production claims.

## Hashtag strategy

- **Core LinkedIn:** `#PaymentOperations` `#PaymentReconciliation` `#FintechOperations`
- **Role-specific LinkedIn:** `#PaymentsEngineering` `#SettlementOperations` `#FinancialOperations`
- **Broad, used selectively:** `#Fintech` `#Payments`
- **Research ledger:** `#TrustLedgerResearch`
- **X:** use no more than two relevant tags per post.

Hashtags are discovery aids, not qualification evidence. A reply counts only after the respondent's
role and operating context are verified.

## Before publishing

1. Publish from the founder's profile first; people respond to a person testing a belief more readily
   than to a new corporate logo.
2. Use this headline in the founder profile or Featured section:
   `Building TrustLedger · Researching how payment teams explain money when systems disagree`.
3. Do not link to `localhost`, imply `/showcase` is publicly hosted or attach a product deck to the
   first contact.
4. Add every published item and resulting conversation to `pilot/social-exposure-tracker.csv`.
5. Move a public reply into a private message before discussing company-specific incidents or data.

## Repurposing strategy

One real, anonymised incident becomes:

1. a LinkedIn chronology of the disagreement;
2. an X thread describing each source state;
3. a one-page source-map diagram;
4. a short founder video asking where the evidence is incomplete;
5. a community question about the unresolved operational step;
6. a follow-up post publishing the operator's correction;
7. a newsletter section comparing the current workflow with the proposed evidence structure;
8. a poll about recurrence or source precedence;
9. a private message requesting a second practitioner perspective; and
10. an entry in the market-gate evidence ledger.

Do not repurpose company-specific facts without explicit permission. Do not turn one incident into a
claim about the whole market.

## Engagement playbook

### Reply qualification

Within 24 hours of a relevant response, ask privately:

> Thank you. To make sure I do not count general interest as evidence: does your work involve at
> least two payment providers, rails or banking partners, and are you close enough to the workflow
> to reconstruct a recent discrepancy? If yes, would a 20-minute research call be reasonable?

### Public reply to “we already solve this with spreadsheets”

> That is exactly the counter-evidence I need. Would you be willing to explain one recent incident
> and where the spreadsheet/provider workflow was sufficient? I will not pitch the product.

### Public reply to “show me the product”

> Happy to show one synthetic reconstruction after I understand the real workflow. I do not want the
> interface to prime your description of the problem.

### Public reply containing sensitive details

> Thank you—please remove any customer or transaction identifiers from the public thread. I will
> message you so we can keep the discussion anonymised.
